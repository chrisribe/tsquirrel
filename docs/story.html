🐿️ A little deeper

The $999 laptop got the quieter upgrade

A closer look at the October 2024 announcement · 2 min read

Buried near the end of Apple's MacBook Pro announcement was a change to a different laptop: the M2 and M3 MacBook Air would start with 16GB of memory instead of 8GB. The Air lineup's starting price stayed at $999. No new chip required. [1]

That put the cheapest Air $600 below the new entry MacBook Pro, with the same starting amount of memory. It did not make them equivalent machines. It did mean that buying a Pro just to avoid an 8GB base configuration was no longer necessary.

There is a practical catch when comparing discounts: the name “M2 MacBook Air” does not tell you whether a listing has 8GB or 16GB. If you find a cheaper 8GB configuration, compare it with the new $999 baseline—not just the seller's claimed saving.

And “MacBook Pro” doesn't mean “M4 Pro”

The $1,599 laptop used the base M4 chip and Thunderbolt 4. Thunderbolt 5 was reserved for M4 Pro and M4 Max models; the 14-inch M4 Pro started at $1,999. If you were buying specifically for Thunderbolt 5 equipment, the relevant starting price was $400 higher than the headline price. [1]

One more reason not to rush: Apple said its first Apple Intelligence features also supported Macs with M1 or later. An existing Apple-silicon Mac did not need an M4 upgrade merely to become eligible. [1]

Our read: before comparing benchmark scores, check three things—the memory in the actual listing, the chip behind the “Pro” name, and which feature your current Mac really lacks. Those distinctions could change the purchase more than the headline does.
